The Founders

Two collectors.
One obsession with
craft and quiet.

Marieke Meulendijks and Maickel Weyers founded Róhe in 2021 from their home in Amsterdam, overlooking the Vondelpark. They are collectors by nature. Art, furniture, objects, relationships with makers. Their home and their brand are built on the same instinct: surround yourself with things made with care, by people who understand what care means.

Marieke comes from fashion and design. Maickel from a background in construction. What they share is a belief in the long-lasting over the immediate, the considered over the convenient. Róhe began as an extension of that shared sensibility and has not drifted from it.

The Craft

Exceptional Italian fabrics.
Uncompromising quality.
Always.

Every Róhe collection is defined by a rewritten definition of tailoring. The team works to perfect construction and fit through research, draping, innovation, and in-house patternmaking. Craftsmanship is not a finishing touch. It is where every piece begins.

The belted trench coat, for example, began as a vintage trench coat that was deconstructed and rebuilt. The detachable double collar that emerged from that process is now one of Róhe's most recognised pieces. Every Róhe classic carries that kind of history: an idea taken seriously until it became something worth keeping.

Fabrics
Exceptional Italian textiles, chosen for their quality, handfeel, and longevity. Sourced from family manufacturers with whom Róhe has built lasting relationships.
Construction
In-house patternmaking, draping, and innovation. Every silhouette is perfected through research before a sample is cut.
Longevity
Made to be kept. Elevated minimalism and clean construction that improves over time. The opposite of disposable.

The World of Róhe

More than fashion.
A way of curating
your life.

Marieke and Maickel are collectors. Their home in Amsterdam is full of art pieces gathered over years, stone objects made with sculptor friends, furniture chosen for its character and its craft. That sensibility lives in everything Róhe touches. The Objects collection, the art collaborations, the cookbooks. Róhe is a world, not just a wardrobe.

On craft as
the foundation
of everything.

"Craftsmanship is key in building collections." For Maickel, this is not an aesthetic statement. It is a working method. The ribbed wool knitted cardigan he calls his favourite piece is made by Ley Tricot, a family manufacturer with whom Róhe has grown over years of shared commitment.

The materials we choose have a major impact on the quality and thus the lifespan of the clothing. A double-face wool jacket with a seamless finish done by hand. A cardigan versatile enough for the office and the bike ride home. Built not for a season but for however long it takes to become a favourite.

Copywriter's Notes
What changed and why

The Opening Statement

The live Brand page opens with: "The Róhe philosophy centers on an infinite dialogue of quiet confidence, where craft, innovation, and an artistic community converge." That sentence is a moodboard description, not a brand voice. It tells you what to think before giving you anything to feel. The rewrite opens with the thing Marieke actually said in the Liberty London interview: "For me it is important that clothes give me a sense of calm empowerment. Strong feeling without being too loud." That sentence is the entire brand. It belongs at the top of the page.

The Founders Section

The current Brand page shows a photo of Marieke and Maickel with a caption. There is no copy that tells you who they are as people, what shaped them, or why Róhe is the inevitable result of their particular sensibilities. The Net-a-Porter home feature on Marieke reveals something important: they are obsessive collectors of art, furniture and objects. That collecting instinct is what gave Róhe its Objects range, its art collaborations, its cookbooks. The brand is a world, not just a wardrobe. The rewrite says so.

The Craft Section

The live Brand page mentions "exceptional Italian fabrics and uncompromising quality" but never shows the work behind those words. The Liberty interview reveals how Maickel and Marieke actually build a collection: through research, draping, and in-house patternmaking. The trench coat, for example, began as a vintage coat that was deconstructed and rebuilt. That story is the best single demonstration of what Róhe means by craftsmanship. The rewrite puts it on the brand page where it can earn belief rather than simply claim it.

The Newsletter Capture

The current Róhe site has no newsletter capture visible on the Brand page. For a brand that has built an entire world of objects, art, furniture, and meaningful collaborations alongside the clothing, the newsletter is the single most important touchpoint for the collector-customer who wants to stay close to that world. The rewrite positions it accordingly: "For those who collect, curate, and keep." That is the Róhe customer in one phrase. The newsletter copy should say so.
